What is Taoism?

Taoism, as China’s indigenous religion, is deeply rooted in the fertile soil of Chinese civilization and has endured for thousands of years. It is not only a religious belief, but also an important part of the traditional culture of the Chinese nation, containing rich philosophical thinking, moral concepts, health wisdom and artistic aesthetics. With “Taoism” as its core, Taoism pursues the harmonious coexistence of man and nature and advocates a lifestyle of doing nothing and living in accordance with nature, which has had a far-reaching influence on future generations.

Historical Background of Taoism

The formation and development of Taoism can be traced back to the pre-Qin period of ancient China. Its ideological origins are complex and varied, blending the essence of ideas from various schools of thought, such as Taoism, Confucianism, Mohism, Yin-Yangism, and Divine Immortality. At the end of the Eastern Han Dynasty, Zhang Daoling founded the Tao of the Heavenly Masters, marking the formal establishment of Taoism as an organized religious entity. Since then, Taoism has undergone differentiation and integration during the Wei, Jin, and North and South Dynasties, and flourished during the Tang and Song Dynasties until its stable development in the Ming and Qing Dynasties, forming the pluralistic Taoist system that we see today. The history of Taoism is a magnificent epic that constantly explores the truth of the universe and pursues the transcendence of life.

Core Ideology of Taoism

The core idea of Taoism, in short, is that “Taoism follows nature”. The “Tao” here is the origin and fundamental law of all things in the universe, which is both invisible and omnipresent, and is the fundamental basis for the creation, change and demise of all things. The “Law of Nature” means that people should follow the laws of nature and not forcefully intervene in order to achieve inner peace and harmony in the universe. Taoism believes that by cultivating the body and mind, one can achieve unity with Tao and realize the eternity and transcendence of life.

Basic beliefs of Taoism

1. The belief system of Taoism

The belief system of Taoism is large and complex, including the worship of the Tao, the belief in deities and immortals, the understanding of yin and yang and the five elements, and the practice of ritual activities such as charms and jiao-festival. Taoists believe that through pious beliefs and correct cultivation methods, they can communicate with heaven and earth, obtain the shelter of the gods and immortals, and ultimately achieve immortality and immortality.

2. Taoism’s Concept of Immortality

The concept of immortality is one of the core beliefs of Taoism. Taoism believes that through cultivation, people can transcend the mortal world and become immortal beings with long life and great powers. These immortals are either historical saints and sages, monks who have attained the Way, or nature spirits and ghosts, who reside in heavenly sanctuaries and possess extraordinary power and wisdom. Taoists seek to achieve the same state by worshipping the immortals and learning their practices.

3. The Pursuit of Cultivating Immortality

Cultivation to become immortal is the highest pursuit of Taoists. Taoism believes that human life is limited, but through cultivation, one can prolong life and even achieve immortality. There are various ways to cultivate immortality, including alchemy and medicine, taking qi to dispel grain, sitting in meditation, and doing good deeds and accumulating virtues. These methods of cultivation aim to purify the mind, strengthen the body, and ultimately reach the realm of unity with the Tao and transcendence to sainthood.

Taoist Teachings and Classics

1. Explanation of Taoism’s Teachings

(1) The Doctrine of Morality: Taoism emphasizes the importance of morality, which is the foundation of cultivation. Taoists should abide by the moral norms of “loyalty, filial piety, benevolence, faith, righteousness, and peace”, and take it as their duty to accumulate virtues and do good deeds, and to cultivate themselves, their families, their country, and the whole world.

(2) Theory of Health Care: Taoism emphasizes health care and believes that physical and mental health is the prerequisite for cultivation and immortality. Taoist health care methods are diverse, including dietary regimen, exercise, breathing and exhaling, psychological adjustment, etc., aiming to harmonize yin and yang, dredge the meridians and strengthen the body.

(3) The idea of the unity of man and nature: Taoism believes that man and nature is an inseparable whole, people should comply with the laws of nature and live in harmony with nature. The idea of the unity of heaven and man is embodied in many aspects of Taoism’s cultivation methods, lifestyles, and artistic creations.

2. Classics and Commentaries

There are many Taoist classics, the most famous of which are Tao Te Ching, Zhuangzi, Taiping Jing, and Zhouyi Sen Tong Qi. Tao Te Ching is the fundamental classic of Taoism, written by Lao Zi, which expounds the mystery of Tao and the essentials of cultivation; while Zhuang Zi, with its unique philosophical thinking and literary style, demonstrates Taoism’s spirituality of freedom and transcendence of the secular world. Classics such as the Taiping Jing and the Zhouyi Sen Tongqi provide in-depth expositions and explanations of Taoist cultivation methods, alchemy, and the doctrines of yin and yang and the five elements.

Taoist Cultivation

Taoist cultivation is an important way for Taoists to pursue immortality and become immortal. There are many different ways to practice Taoism, including meditation, refining qi and shen, doing good deeds and accumulating virtues, and taking pills and medicines. Sitting meditation, aimed at quieting the mind, reconciling yin and yang, communicating with heaven and earth; refining qi and god, through breathing and exhaling, intention to guide the method, enhance the body’s vital energy, improve the quality of the body; do good and accumulate virtue, is an important part of Taoist practice, through doing good deeds, accumulation of merit to purify the mind, enhance the realm; alchemy and medicine, is a more special method of Taoist cultivation, aimed at taking pills to strengthen the body, prolong life.

Taoism, not only contains profound philosophical thoughts, but also gives birth to a colorful health and fitness art, Taoist Kung Fu. Taoist Kung Fu, also known as Taoist Kung Fu or Neidanjutsu, is a unique practice system that combines physical and mental cultivation, philosophical thinking and life science.

I. The Origin and Development of Taoist Kungfu

The origin of Taoist Kung Fu can be traced back to the Taoist doctrine in the pre-Qin period, especially the thoughts of Laozi and Zhuangzi. In Tao Te Ching, Lao Tzu put forward the idea of “Taoism is based on nature”, emphasizing obedience to the laws of nature and the pursuit of inner peace and freedom, while Zhuang Tzu depicted a spiritual realm of transcendence and forgetfulness of the world in his “Journey to the Prowess of the World”. These ideas laid the philosophical foundation for the formation of Taoist Kung Fu.

After the Han Dynasty, with the establishment and development of Taoist organizations, Taoist Kung Fu began to gradually form a system. In the early days, Taoist cultivation was mostly focused on exoteric elixir, i.e., the making of elixirs in order to achieve immortality. However, because of the many risks involved in the practice of external elixir and the difficulty of verifying its effects, the practice gradually shifted to the safer and more effective internal elixir. Internal alchemy emphasizes the harmonization of yin and yang through self-cultivation to achieve harmony and unity of body and mind.

During the Tang and Song dynasties, Taoist internal alchemy reached its peak of development. Taoist masters such as Lu Dongbin and Zhang Boduan systematically organized and elaborated on the art of internal alchemy, forming a more complete cultivation system. Among them, Zhang Boduan’s Wujin Zhuan is regarded as a classic work of internal alchemy, which had a profound influence on later generations.

II. The Theoretical Foundations of Taoist Kung Fu

The theoretical foundation of Taoist Kung Fu mainly includes the doctrine of Yin-Yang and Five Elements, the idea of the unity of heaven and man, and the Taoist concept of doing nothing.

The doctrine of Yin-Yang and Five Elements is one of the theoretical pillars of Taoist Kung Fu. Yin and Yang represent the two aspects of unity of opposites, such as heaven and earth, sun and moon, cold and heat. The five elements, on the other hand, refer to the five basic forms of matter or energy – gold, wood, water, fire and earth – which are mutually constraining and transforming, and constitute the basic framework of all things in the universe. Taoist Kung Fu believes that the human body is also a small universe, which also follows the laws of yin and yang and the five elements. By harmonizing yin and yang and balancing the five elements during cultivation, harmony and health of body and mind can be achieved.

The idea of the unity of heaven and man is the core concept of Taoist Kung Fu. Taoism believes that man is a part of heaven and earth, and that there is a close connection between him and everything in the universe. Through cultivation, one can gradually recognize and conform to the laws of nature, and realize the realm of breathing with heaven and earth and sharing the same destiny. This realm is not only reflected in physical strength and longevity, but also in spiritual transcendence and freedom.

The Taoist concept of ruling by doing nothing is also reflected in Taoist kung fu. Instead of doing nothing, Taoism emphasizes the importance of following nature and not acting arbitrarily. In the process of cultivation, Taoist Kung Fu advocates the use of tranquility and softness to counteract external disturbances and changes through inner peace and tranquility.

C. Taoist Kung Fu Cultivation Methods

The cultivation methods of Taoist Kung Fu mainly include regulating breath, refining qi, nourishing spirit and refining form.

Breath regulation is the foundation of Taoist Kung Fu practice. By adjusting the rhythm and depth of breathing, the body’s qi and blood can be harmonized, and the body’s immunity and self-healing ability can be strengthened. There are various ways to regulate breathing, such as deep breathing and reverse abdominal breathing.

Refining qi is the core of Taoist kung fu. Refining Qi is not simply a matter of luck or holding one’s breath, but the purification and sublimation of the body’s vital energy through specific cultivation methods. The process of refining qi needs to be gradual, from the preliminary refining of essence and transforming qi to the advanced refining of qi and transforming god, and each stage requires a long period of perseverance and hard work.

Nurturing the spirit is an integral part of Taoist kung fu. Nourishing the spirit not only means maintaining the spirit and regulating the emotions, but also includes raising the level of consciousness and developing the potential. Through methods such as meditation and quiet sitting, one can gradually reach a state where the mind is free of distracting thoughts and the mind can forget itself, thus realizing spiritual transcendence and freedom.

Form-refining is one of the more specific cultivation methods in Taoist Kung Fu. Refining the form is mainly done through specific postures and movements to exercise the flexibility and coordination of the body. Boxing and gong methods such as Taijiquan and Baduanjin are common means of refining form in Taoist Kung Fu. These techniques not only strengthen the body, but also help the practitioner to achieve a state of unity of mind and body.

The Cultural Influence and Role of Taoism in China

The relationship between Taoism and traditional Chinese culture

Taoism and traditional Chinese culture are closely connected and mutually influential. Taoist thought permeates all levels of traditional Chinese culture, including philosophy, literature, art, medicine, architecture and so on. Taoism’s doctrine of yin and yang and the five elements had a profound influence on ancient Chinese philosophy and medicine.

Influence of Taoism in Philosophy, Literature, Art and Other Fields

Philosophy: Taoist philosophy takes “Tao” as its core, emphasizing the ideas of “doing nothing” and “following nature”. This philosophical concept has had a profound impact on ancient Chinese philosophy. For example, the wisdom in the Tao Te Ching has been widely quoted and developed by later philosophers, and has become an important cornerstone of ancient Chinese philosophy. Taoist philosophy also advocates the cosmology of “unity of heaven and mankind”, believing that man and nature should coexist harmoniously, a concept that still serves as a revelation to modern ecological philosophy.

Literature: Taoist culture has enriched the themes and expressions of ancient literature. Many literati were influenced by Taoist thought and created poems and songs with strong Taoist colors. For example, Li Bai, a poet of the Tang Dynasty, often revealed in his works his longing for Taoist fairyland and his deep understanding of Taoist philosophy. In addition, Taoist stories and myths also provide rich material for literary creation.

Artistic field: The influence of Taoism on art is manifested in a number of ways. In painting, Taoist landscapes and figure paintings became one of the most important genres of ancient Chinese painting. For example, the Southern Song artist Ma Yuan’s “Treading Songs” embodies the Taoist pursuit of “immortal mountains and pavilions”. In terms of music and dance, the music and dance in Taoist rituals have unique artistic charms, such as the “immortal music” in Taoist music, whose melody is melodious and far-reaching, which had an important impact on ancient Chinese music culture.

The Status of Taoism in Folk Beliefs and Customs

Taoism occupies a pivotal position in folk beliefs. It is deeply rooted in folklore and closely connected with people’s lives, forming many folk beliefs and customs with local characteristics. For example, Taoist rituals such as “praying for blessings and eliminating disasters” and “exorcising evil spirits and avoiding misfortune” are widely spread among the people, and have become an important way for people to seek spiritual solace in the face of life’s difficulties. In addition, Taoism also influences the formation of folk festivals and customs, such as “firecrackers” during the Spring Festival, “posting Spring Festival couplets” and other customs, which are related to the concept of Taoism to drive away and avoid evil spirits.

Modern Development and Social Significance of Taoism

Practice and Inheritance of Taoism in Contemporary Society: With the development of the times, Taoism is constantly innovating and reforming while maintaining its traditional essence. The modern Taoist community actively advocates the inheritance and development of Taoist culture by organizing Taoist cultural festivals, Taoist forums and other activities. At the same time, Taoism also actively participates in social welfare programs, such as charitable aid and environmental protection, demonstrating the positive role of Taoism in contemporary society.

The connection between Taoism and environmental protection, health and its positive contribution to society: Taoism emphasizes the “unity of heaven and mankind”, believing that man and nature should coexist harmoniously. This concept is widely recognized and practiced in modern society. The green lifestyle and environmental protection concepts advocated by Taoism are of great significance in promoting the construction of an ecological civilization and sustainable development. In addition, Taoism also focuses on health and fitness, and its unique methods of health care and medical technology play a positive role in improving people’s health and preventing diseases.

Dissemination and Exchanges of Taoist Culture in the Context of Globalization: In the context of globalization, Taoist culture is also going global. Through activities such as the International Taoist Forum and the Taoist Cultural Festival, Taoist culture has been widely disseminated and exchanged. This not only enhances cultural understanding and friendship among different countries and regions, but also promotes the internationalization of Taoist culture.

Conclusion of Taoism

To summarize, Taoism, as an indigenous religion of China, has been influential across many fields, including philosophy, literature and art, and has occupied an important position in folk beliefs and customs. In contemporary society, Taoism not only maintains its traditional essence, but also continues to innovate and reform, actively engages in social welfare, and makes positive contributions to the harmonious development of society and the construction of ecological civilization. At the same time, the moral ethics and values in Taoist culture also provide valuable ideological resources and practical guidance for modern society. Therefore, we should pay more attention to the inheritance and development of Taoist culture, so that this valuable cultural heritage can shine more brilliantly in the context of the new era.
